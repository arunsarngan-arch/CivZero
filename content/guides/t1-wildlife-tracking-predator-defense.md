In a primitive or post-collapse environment, wildlife is both your primary source of high-density protein and your most lethal environmental hazard. Surviving among wild fauna requires three distinct disciplines: reading subtle ground disturbances (spoor tracking) to find game, understanding predator psychology to survive apex encounters, and treating venomous envenomation.

### Section 1: Zoological Spoor Tracking & Gait Analysis

Animals leave four classes of sign: prints (tracks), droppings (scat), feeding evidence (browsing/kill sites), and territorial markers (scratches, rubs, scent posts).

#### 1. Track Identification Matrix

| Animal Family | Claw Marks Visible? | Toe Count & Shape | Heel Pad Profile | Gait & Behavior |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Canines** (Wolf, Coyote, Dog) | **Yes** (prominent blunt tips) | 4 oval toes, tight arrangement | Triangular, single anterior lobe, double indented posterior base | Straight line travel; rear foot registers directly inside front track to conserve energy. |
| **Felines** (Cougar, Lynx, Bobcat) | **No** (retractable claws protected) | 4 teardrop toes, leading toe creates asymmetry | Large trapezoid with two anterior lobes and three posterior lobes | Wandering, stealthy stalking path; tracks avoid water, mud, and dry leaves. |
| **Ursids** (Grizzly & Black Bears) | **Yes** (long, heavy digging claws) | 5 toes, broad human-like foot | Massive flat heel pad; hind foot resembles human bare footprint | Plantigrade walker; toes arc across front; grizzly claws extend 5–10 cm ahead of toes. |
| **Ungulates** (Deer, Elk, Boar) | N/A (hoof keratin) | 2 cloven hard cleavers (hooves) | Dewclaws appear behind hoof in deep mud or high-speed gallop | Deer hooves taper to sharp point; wild boar hooves are blunt, wide, with rounded tips. |
| **Mustelids** (Otter, Badger, Wolverine) | **Yes** (sharp curved claws) | 5 toes, semi-plantigrade | Curved C-shaped heel pad | Diagonal loper or bounder; body slides visible in snow or riverbanks. |
| **Lagomorphs** (Rabbits, Hares) | **Yes** (fine point nails) | 4 front, 4 elongated rear | Heavily furred pads obscure clear toe pads | Distinct "Y" track: two small front feet land one behind the other, two large hind feet swing ahead. |

#### 2. Determining Spoor Age (Track Degradation Timeline)

### Section 2: Large Predator Confrontation & Defensive Protocols

When encountering apex carnivores, physical running triggers the mammalian predatory chase instinct ($>60\text{ km/h}$ acceleration). Follow species-specific behavioral rules:

```
        PREDATOR RESPONSE MATRIX: BEAR vs. COUGAR vs. WOLF
        
         GRIZZLY BEAR                     BLACK BEAR / COUGAR
    [DEFENSIVE CHARGE]                   [PREDATORY STALKING]
    ─────────────────                    ────────────────────
    • DO NOT RUN                         • DO NOT RUN OR PLAY DEAD
    • DO NOT MAKE EYE CONTACT            • MAINTAIN INTENSE EYE CONTACT
    • Stand ground; speak softly         • Stand tall, shout, open jacket
    • If physical contact:               • FIGHT BACK WITH WEAPONS
      PLAY DEAD FACE DOWN                  Target eyes, snout, throat!
```

#### 1. Brown / Grizzly Bear (*Ursus arctos*)
- **Physical Traits**: Distinct muscular hump between shoulder blades; dished facial profile; long ($5\text{--}10\text{ cm}$) light-colored claws.
- **Encounter Type**: Usually **defensive** (protecting cubs, carcass, or startled in dense brush).
- **Protocol**:
  1. Never run. Talk in calm, low monotone voice while slowly waving arms to identify yourself as human.
  2. If the bear bluffs charges (ears forward, loud huffing, slapping ground with paws), stand your ground. Most charges halt within 5 meters.
  3. If attack is imminent, **drop flat onto stomach, interlace fingers behind neck, spread elbows and legs wide** to prevent being flipped over. Keep backpack on to absorb bites. Wait until bear leaves area completely before moving.

#### 2. American Black Bear (*Ursus americanus*)
- **Physical Traits**: Straight nose bridge line; no shoulder hump; short ($2\text{--}4\text{ cm}$) dark curved claws.
- **Encounter Type**: Often **predatory** or habituated scavenging.
- **Protocol**:
  1. **NEVER PLAY DEAD**. Black bear attacks are almost always predatory.
  2. Make yourself appear massive: stand on logs, raise arms, spread jackets.
  3. Shout aggressively. Throw rocks and heavy branches.
  4. If attacked, **fight back with total ferocity**. Strike eyes, kick snout, stab with knives or spears. Black bears yield when their target demonstrates painful resistance.

#### 3. Mountain Lion / Cougar (*Puma concolor*)
- **Stalking Behavior**: Silent ambush from behind or elevated rocky ledges; target neck and cervical spine.
- **Protocol**:
  1. **Never turn your back and never crouch or bend over** (crouching mimics quadrupedal prey).
  2. Maintain unblinking eye contact. Slowly raise arms above head to increase silhouette height.
  3. Speak in a loud, commanding, assertive voice.
  4. If animal crouches, twitches tail, and pins ears back, charge is imminent: throw objects, brandish heavy sticks, and prepare to deliver vertical downward blows to its skull.

#### 4. Grey Wolf (*Canis lupus*) & Feral Canine Packs
- **Pack Tactics**: Flankers encircle target while point animal tests defenses; attacks target hamstrings and throat.
- **Protocol**:
  1. Back up against an impassable barrier (cliff face, boulder, large tree) to prevent rear flanking.
  2. Fire brandishing: wild canines fear flame and sparks. Swing torches or ignite dry grass.
  3. Do not run or look submissive. Shout aggressively and advance toward isolated flankers to break pack cohesion.

### Section 3: Global Venomous Snake Taxonomy & Envenomation Care

Serpents fall into two distinct venomous families with fundamentally different fangs, pupils, and venoms:

```
               VIPERIDAE vs. ELAPIDAE COMPARATIVE ANATOMY
               
    VIPERIDAE (Pit Vipers, Rattlesnakes)    ELAPIDAE (Cobras, Coral Snakes, Mambas)
    • Triangular, arrow-shaped head        • Slender, rounded head (streamlined)
    • Vertical elliptical pupils ("cat eye")• Round circular pupils
    • Deep loreal heat-sensing pit         • No loreal pits between eye and nostril
    • Long, retractable hinged fangs       • Short, fixed erect front fangs
    • HEMOTOXIC / CYTOTOXIC VENOM          • NEUROTOXIC VENOM (Paralysis)
```

| Snake Family | Representative Global Species | Venom Pathology | Clinical Symptoms | Critical First-Aid Protocol |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Viperidae** (Pit Vipers & True Vipers) | Rattlesnake, Copperhead, Cottonmouth, Bushmaster, European Adder, Russell's Viper | **Hemotoxins, Myotoxins, Cytotoxins**: destructures blood vessels, lyses red blood cells, causes severe tissue necrosis. | Excruciating local pain, rapid purple edema, blood blistering, systemic hemorrhaging, coagulopathy. | **DO NOT APPLY TOURNIQUET**. Immobilize limb at heart level. Clean bite with water. Wrap loose splint. Tourniquets cause massive localized gangrene and limb amputation. |
| **Elapidae** (Elapids) | Coral Snake, Black/Green Mamba, King Cobra, Indian Krait, Taipan, Tiger Snake | **Neurotoxins**: blocks acetylcholine receptors at post-synaptic neuromuscular junctions. | Minimal local pain/swelling; ptosis (drooping eyelids), slurred speech, generalized muscle flaccidity, respiratory failure. | **PRESSURE IMMOBILIZATION TECHNIQUE (PIT)**. Apply broad elastic bandage (10–15 cm wide) from toes/fingers up entire limb with tension of a sprained ankle wrap. Splint rigidly. Halts lymphatic flow to vital organs. |

#### Inviolable Rules of Snakebite Field Care:
- **NEVER** cut the bite puncture wounds with a razor blade.
- **NEVER** attempt oral suction (mouth bacteria causes catastrophic wound infection; venom is not extractable).
- **NEVER** apply ice or cryotherapy (accelerates tissue gangrene).
- **NEVER** use electric shocks or car battery jumper cables.
- **ALWAYS** keep the victim completely motionless; physical activity doubles the rate of systemic venom distribution through the lymphatic channels.

### Section 4: Primitive Trapping & Snare Mechanics

A survival snare works 24 hours a day while conserving critical human calories. Traps rely on hair-trigger mechanics:

#### 1. The Figure-4 Deadfall Trap
The definitive trigger for deadfall stones (up to 20 kg) to crush rodents, squirrels, and small game:

```
                 FIGURE-4 DEADFALL TRIGGER SCHEMATIC
                 
                       Heavy Deadfall Stone
                         \
                          \
                           \
                            \ (Vertical Post A)
                             \  │
            (Diagonal Arm B)  \ │
                   ╲           \│
                    ╲           │
                     ╲    ┌─────┼────────────────(Horizontal Bait Stick C)
                      ╲  ┌┘     │               [Bait on tip]
                       ╲ │      │
                        ╲│      │
                         ▼      │
                                Ground
```

- **Notch Construction**:
  - **Stick A (Vertical)**: Flat base, beveled chisel top, square horizontal notch cut midway down one side.
  - **Stick B (Diagonal)**: Top notch rests beneath deadfall stone; bottom notch mates with trigger stick C.
  - **Stick C (Bait Stick)**: Horizontal stick with rear notch mating into vertical post A and diagonal stick B; extends underneath stone with fresh bait skewered on end.
  - **Physics**: When game nibbles bait on stick C, it pivots out of the vertical notch, releasing diagonal arm B, instantly dropping the stone.

#### 2. The Spring-Pole Snare (High-Yield Small Game)
Uses the stored potential elastic energy of a bent green sapling to lift small animals (rabbits, hares) into the air, preventing predator scavenging:

```
                  SPRING-POLE SNARE SCHEMATIC
                  
     Bent Sapling (Stored Tension)
           ╲
            ╲
             ╲
              ╲
               │ (Cordage)
               │
              [Toggle Pin] ─────── Mated to Ground Stake Notches
               │
               ├─── Wire/Cordage Noose (4-finger width loop, 4 fingers off ground)
               │
            Game Run Trail Between Guide Sticks
```

- **Snare Wire Loop Sizing**:
  - **Rabbit / Hare**: Noose diameter $10\text{ cm}$ (4 inches); suspended $7\text{ cm}$ (3 inches) off the ground surface.
  - **Squirrel / Possum**: Noose diameter $6\text{--}8\text{ cm}$; suspended $4\text{ cm}$ off log runway.
  - **Placement**: Never place traps in random woods; locate high-density animal runs (packed narrow runs through brush, under fences, or between fallen timber).
