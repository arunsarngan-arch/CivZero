## Critical Indications
Exsanguination from severed femoral or brachial arteries causes irreversible shock and cardiac arrest in **60 to 180 seconds**. When direct pressure fails on extremity arterial spurts (bright pulsing red blood), an improvised windlass tourniquet must be applied immediately proximal to the wound.

## Protocol Steps
1. **Band Selection:** Use broad non-elastic cloth (triangular bandage, scarf, strip of canvas) **at least 5 cm (2 inches) wide**. Never use wire, cord, or thin shoe laces, which sheer through skin and muscle.
2. **Placement:** Wrap 5–7 cm proximal (above) the bleeding site. **Never place directly over a joint** (elbow or knee); if the wound is just below a joint, position immediately above it.
3. **Half Knot & Windlass Insert:** Tie a half-knot, place a rigid stick or metal rod (15–20 cm long, unbreakable under torque) over the knot, and secure with a full square knot over the rod.
4. **Torsion (Twisting):** Twist the windlass rod clockwise until arterial pulsating stops and the distal pulse is extinguished.
5. **Lock Windlass:** Secure the rod firmly to the limb using the tails of the band or a second strip of cloth so it cannot untwist.
6. **Marking:** Record the exact application time ("T=HH:MM") on the patient's forehead using blood, charcoal, or marker. Do not loosen or remove until surgical closure is ready.
