## Botanical Principles
Modern hybrid seeds (F1) revert to unpredictable, non-viable, or barren traits when saved. Long-term civilization survival demands **Open-Pollinated (OP) or Heirloom varieties**. Seeds must be harvested at full biological maturity (beyond culinary ripeness), dried to **below 8% internal moisture content**, and sealed in airtight oxygen-depleted containers.

## Seed Saving Protocol
1. **Genetic Isolation:** Prevent cross-pollination between brassicas, squash, and corn by maintaining minimum physical isolation distances (minimum 50–100 meters) or hand-pollinating and bagging blossoms.
2. **Fermentation (Wet Seeds - Tomato, Squash, Melons):** Scoop pulp and seeds into a glass jar with water. Allow to ferment for 48–72 hours until a layer of white fungus forms. This breaks down germination inhibitors and sterilizes surface pathogens (bacterial canker). Viable seeds sink to the bottom.
3. **Desiccation:** Spread rinsed seeds in a single layer over brown craft paper or window screens in an airy, shaded location. **Never heat above 35°C (95°F)** or the embryo will die.
4. **Viability Test (The 10-Seed Germination Assay):** Roll 10 seeds inside a damp cloth inside a warm dark pouch for 7 days. If 8 germinate, your lot has an 80% viability rating.
5. **Airtight Storage:** Pack dry seeds with an equal volume of sifted, dry wood ash or powdered clay (acting as natural desiccant) in sealed mason jars or soldered tin canisters. Bury in an underground earthen cellar at 10°C–14°C.
