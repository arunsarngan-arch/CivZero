In survival, perimeter defense, hunting, and post-collapse order, firearms represent the dominant mechanical tool for personal and communal defense. Following infrastructure breakdown, survivors will encounter diverse stockpiles of military surplus and civilian sporting arms manufactured across different centuries, nations, and caliber standards.

Attempting to chamber an incorrect cartridge (e.g., forcing a 7.62x51mm NATO round into a .30-06 chamber, or mixing 9x18mm Makarov with 9x19mm Parabellum) causes catastrophic chamber explosions, permanent weapon destruction, and fatal shrapnel injury.

This manual provides an **encyclopedic reference catalog of international small arms, their country of origin, standard ammunition calibers, mechanical actions, and headstamp identification**.


### Section 2: Mechanical Operating Actions

Understanding the internal mechanical action is essential for diagnosis, cleaning, and clearing malfunctions:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 1. BOLT-ACTION      → Manual mechanical bolt rotation  │
│ 2. DIRECT IMPINGE   → Expanding gas tube pushes bolt   │
│ 3. GAS PISTON       → Gas pushes rod (Short/Long stroke│
│ 4. BLOWBACK         → Cartridge recoil pushes slide    │
│ 5. RECOIL OPERATION → Barrel & slide recoil together   │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Manual Bolt-Action:**
   - *Mechanism:* The operator manually lifts the bolt handle, pulls it rearward to extract and eject the spent case, pushes it forward to strip a fresh cartridge from the magazine, and locks the lugs into the receiver by rotating down.
   - *Characteristics:* Maximum mechanical reliability, zero gas energy bleed (highest muzzle velocity), impervious to mud and dirt. *Examples:* Mauser 98, Mosin-Nagant, Lee-Enfield, Remington 700.
2. **Direct Gas Impingement (DI):**
   - *Mechanism:* High-pressure gas behind the firing bullet bleeds through a barrel port into a narrow gas tube directed straight back into the bolt carrier inside the receiver, driving it rearward to cycle.
   - *Characteristics:* Highly accurate (low reciprocating mass), but deposits hot carbon residue directly into the chamber area, requiring routine lubrication. *Examples:* M16, M4, AR-15 family.
3. **Gas Piston (Long-Stroke vs. Short-Stroke):**
   - *Long-Stroke:* The operating piston rod is physically welded to the bolt carrier, moving as a single heavy unit. Immensely robust, powers through sand and ice. *Examples:* AK-47, AKM, M1 Garand.
   - *Short-Stroke:* Gas pushes a light tappet rod that strikes the bolt carrier with a brief impulse, then returns under spring tension. Cleaner running than direct impingement. *Examples:* SKS, FN FAL, HK416, SVD Dragunov.
4. **Short-Recoil Tilting Barrel (Browning Action):**
   - *Mechanism:* Common to almost all modern semi-automatic handguns. Upon firing, the barrel and slide remain locked together for 3–5 mm of rearward travel until chamber pressure drops safely; the barrel then tilts downward on a link or cam, unlocking the slide to cycle. *Examples:* 1911, Glock, Browning Hi-Power, CZ 75.

### Section 3: Major Global Platforms Cataloged by Country of Origin

#### 1. United States of America (USA)
* **M16 / M4 / AR-15 Family ($5.56\times 45\text{mm}$):** Modular aluminum upper and lower receivers, rotating bolt, multi-position collapsible stock. Magazine: standard STANAG 30-round box.
* **M1911 / M1911A1 (.45 ACP):** Single-action semi-automatic pistol, all-steel construction, grip safety and manual thumb safety, 7-round single-stack box magazine.
* **M14 / M1A ($7.62\times 51\text{mm}$):** Rotating bolt, gas piston battle rifle derived from the WWII M1 Garand. 20-round detachable magazine.
* **Remington 870 & Mossberg 500 (12 Gauge):** Pump-action tubular-magazine shotguns. Renowned for ruggedness in rain, mud, and maritime salt spray.

#### 2. Russia & Soviet Union (USSR)
* **AK-47 / AKM ($7.62\times 39\text{mm}$):** Stamped steel receiver (AKM), chrome-lined bore, long-stroke gas piston. Loose manufacturing tolerances allow flawless operation in sand, mud, and sub-zero Siberian frost. 30-round curved steel/bakelite magazine.
* **AK-74 ($5.45\times 39\text{mm}$):** Modernized Kalashnikov with massive two-chamber muzzle brake reducing recoil, chambered in the high-velocity $5.45\text{mm}$ round.
* **SVD Dragunov ($7.62\times 54\text{mmR}$):** Semi-automatic designated marksman rifle, short-stroke gas system, paired with PSO-1 4x optical scope.
* **Makarov PM ($9\times 18\text{mm}$):** Simple blowback double-action service sidearm with only 27 total parts. Extremely durable compact pocket pistol.

#### 3. Germany
* **Mauser Gewehr 98 & Kar98k ($7.92\times 57\text{mm}$):** Controlled-round feed claw extractor, dual front locking lugs. The design blueprint for almost all modern bolt-action hunting and sniper rifles.
* **Heckler & Koch G3 ($7.62\times 51\text{mm}$):** Stamped sheet-metal receiver, roller-delayed blowback system without any gas piston. Fluted chamber allows reliable extraction under extreme fouling.
* **Heckler & Koch MP5 ($9\times 19\text{mm}$):** Closed-bolt roller-delayed submachine gun famous for surgical precision and low recoil.
* **Walther P38 & P99 ($9\times 19\text{mm}$):** Introduced double-action/single-action decocking triggers to military combat handguns.

#### 4. Belgium
* **FN FAL ($7.62\times 51\text{mm}$ NATO):** "The Right Arm of the Free World." Adopted by over 90 sovereign nations during the Cold War. Short-stroke adjustable gas regulator system, tilting bolt.
* **Browning Hi-Power ($9\times 19\text{mm}$):** Designed by John Browning and Dieudonné Saive. The first successful high-capacity "wonder nine" handgun with a 13-round double-stack magazine.
* **FN SCAR (16 / 17) ($5.56\text{mm}$ & $7.62\text{mm}$):** Modern modular combat assault rifle with monolithic aluminum upper receiver and polymer lower.

#### 5. Austria
* **Glock 17 & 19 ($9\times 19\text{mm}$):** High-strength polymer frame, striker-fired "Safe Action" system with no manual external safety levers. Corrosion-proof ferritic nitrocarburizing finish. The global standard for military and police service sidearms.
* **Steyr AUG ($5.56\times 45\text{mm}$):** Bullpup rifle configuration (action and magazine situated behind the trigger), offering a full 508 mm rifle barrel in an ultra-compact carbine length.

#### 6. United Kingdom
* **Lee-Enfield No. 4 Mk I (.303 British):** Rear-locking bolt lugs with short 60° bolt throw, enabling rapid cycling ("the mad minute" of 20–30 aimed rounds/min). 10-round double-column magazine.
* **L85A2 / SA80 ($5.56\times 45\text{mm}$):** British armed forces bullpup service rifle, gas piston operated, optics-centric.

#### 7. Israel
* **IMI Uzi ($9\times 19\text{mm}$):** Open-bolt submachine gun featuring a telescoping bolt that wraps around the breech of the barrel, housing the magazine inside the pistol grip for intuitive reloading.
* **IWI Galil ($5.56\text{mm}$ / $7.62\text{mm}$):** Desert-hardened derivative of the Finnish Valmet and Soviet AK action, featuring a milled receiver and bottle-opener bipod.
* **IWI Tavor ($5.56\times 45\text{mm}$):** Modern bullpup combat rifle optimized for close-quarters urban containment.

#### 8. Italy
* **Beretta 92FS / M9 ($9\times 19\text{mm}$):** Open-slide design virtually immune to stovepipe extraction jams; falling-block locking system. US Military standard sidearm from 1985 to 2017.
* **Benelli M4 Super 90 (12 Gauge):** Auto-regulating gas-operated (ARGO) dual stainless-steel piston semi-automatic tactical shotgun.

#### 9. Czech Republic
* **CZ 75 ($9\times 19\text{mm}$):** Features slide rails riding *inside* the receiver frame (unlike the 1911 or Glock), resulting in tight lockup, low bore axis, and minimal muzzle flip.
* **Vz. 58 ($7.62\times 39\text{mm}$):** Visually resembles an AK-47, but shares zero parts internally; utilizes a striker-fired falling-block action and an exceptionally light milled receiver.

### Section 4: Ammunition Identification & Headstamps

When salvaging loose ammunition from crates or abandoned stockpiles, inspect the **headstamp** engraved on the brass/steel base around the primer:

```
            TOP (Manufacturer / Arsenal Code)
                        "LC"
                         ▲
            ◄───────── [ (•) ] ─────────►
           "NAT0"                 "21"
            Cross              (Year: 2021)
                         ▼
           BOTTOM (Caliber or Date of Batch)
```

1. **NATO Cross Symbol (Circle with a $+$):** Indicates compliance with NATO standardization agreements (STANAG). The cartridge is rated for military chamber pressures and has sealed primers.
2. **Arsenal Codes:**
   - `LC`: Lake City Army Ammunition Plant (USA).
   - `WCC`: Winchester Cartridge Company (USA).
   - `WOLF` / `TULA` / `BARNAUL`: Russian commercial production (frequently polymer-coated or lacquered steel cases).
   - `FN`: Fabrique Nationale (Belgium).
   - `RG`: Radway Green (United Kingdom).
3. **Berdan vs. Boxer Primers:**
   - **Boxer Primers (Standard in US/NATO commercial brass):** Single central flash-hole in the primer pocket. Easily punched out with a decapping pin for hand-reloading.
   - **Berdan Primers (Common in Soviet, Russian, and European military surplus):** Dual offset flash-holes with an integral anvil in the case pocket. Steel-cased Berdan rounds are difficult to reload without specialized hydraulic decappers.

### Section 5: Long-Term Storage & Preservation Protocols

Ammunition has an extraordinarily long shelf life if protected from moisture and heat:
* **The Decomposition Enemy:** Heat and humidity break down the nitrocellulose propellant and lead styphnate/tetracene primary explosive inside the primer cup, causing hang-fires or duds.
* **Storage Protocol:**
  1. Pack ammunition inside heavy-gauge steel ammo cans with clean rubber gaskets.
  2. Include silica gel desiccant packs to maintain internal relative humidity below 30%.
  3. Store in a subterranean cellar or temperature-stable vault below $20^\circ\text{C}$ ($68^\circ\text{F}$).
  4. Ammunition stored sealed in dry, cool conditions remains completely reliable for **50 to 100+ years**.
