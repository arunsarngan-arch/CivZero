### Step 1: Reconstructing the Second (Unit of Time)

Time can be accurately established without clocks using the invariant rotational frequency of planet Earth or a simple gravity pendulum.

#### 1. The Solar Day & Temporal Second
1. The Earth rotates on its axis once every **Mean Solar Day** (from one solar noon meridian to the next).
2. The standard second is mathematically defined as:
   $$1\,\text{Mean Solar Day} = 24\,\text{hours} = 1,440\,\text{minutes} = 86,400\,\text{seconds}$$
   $$\text{Therefore: } 1\,\text{Second} = \frac{1}{86,400} \text{ of a Mean Solar Day}$$

#### 2. The Seconds Pendulum (Empirical Physical Instrument)
A pendulum's period $T$ depends only on its length $L$ and local gravitational acceleration $g$:
$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}$$
* A **"Seconds Pendulum"** is designed to take exactly **$1.000\text{ second}$ per half-swing** (a complete back-and-forth cycle takes $2.000\text{ seconds}$).
* At sea level at $45^\circ$ latitude ($g = 9.80665\,\text{m/s}^2$), the precise length of a seconds pendulum is:
  $$L = \frac{g}{\pi^2} = \frac{9.80665}{(3.14159)^2} \approx 0.9936\,\text{meters}$$
* By constructing a pendulum calibrated to complete exactly 43,200 full swings between two consecutive solar noons, you have established both the true **Second** and the true **Meter** simultaneously!

### Step 2: Reconstructing the Meter (Unit of Length)

Once the second is established via the solar day, the **Meter** is locked into place through the seconds pendulum or the invariant speed of light:

1. **The Seconds Pendulum Definition:**
   A fine wire suspending a dense lead sphere, adjusted so that 3,600 half-swings occur in 1 hour (measured by sundial shadow rate), has a length of **$0.994\,\text{meters}$**. Divide by $994$ to obtain exact millimeters ($1\,\text{mm}$), then multiply by $1,000$ to obtain the true Meter ($1.000\,\text{m}$).
2. **The Invariant Constant Definition (Universal Benchmark):**
   The speed of electromagnetic radiation (light) in vacuum is constant:
   $$c = 299,792,458\,\text{meters per second}$$

### Step 3: Reconstructing the Kilogram (Unit of Mass)

Mass is derived by coupling the newly established **Meter** to the density of pure, distilled liquid water.

#### 1. The Metric Density Link
* Form an open cube whose internal dimensions are exactly $10.0\,\text{cm} \times 10.0\,\text{cm} \times 10.0\,\text{cm}$ ($0.1\,\text{m} \times 0.1\,\text{m} \times 0.1\,\text{m}$).
* The volume enclosed is exactly **$1.0\,\text{cubic decimeter} = 1.0\,\text{Liter}$**.
* Pure distilled water achieves its maximum physical density at **$3.98^\circ\text{C}$** ($39.2^\circ\text{F}$):
  $$\text{Mass of } 1.0\,\text{Liter of pure water at } 4^\circ\text{C} \equiv \mathbf{1.000\,\text{Kilogram}} \quad (1,000\,\text{grams})$$
* **The Gram:** $1.0\,\text{cubic centimeter}$ ($1\,\text{cm}^3$ / $1\,\text{mL}$) of pure water has a mass of exactly **$1.000\,\text{gram}$**.
* **Standard Calibration Weights:** Pour $1,000\,\text{mL}$ of pure cold water into a balanced two-pan balance scale; place lead or brass weights on the opposing pan until perfectly balanced to create durable, non-evaporating metal reference masses.

### Step 4: Reconstructing the Temperature Scale (Celsius & Kelvin)

Temperature is calibrated using the invariant phase transitions of water at standard sea-level atmospheric pressure ($1.0\,\text{atm} = 101.325\,\text{kPa}$):

1. **Zero Point ($0^\circ\text{C}$ / $273.15\,\text{K}$):**
   Submerge a sealed glass tube with a narrow capillary containing mercury or dyed alcohol into an insulated vessel packed with crushed melting freshwater ice. Mark the liquid meniscus level as **$0^\circ\text{C}$**.
2. **Boiling Point ($100^\circ\text{C}$ / $373.15\,\text{K}$):**
   Place the same thermometer into the steam immediately above vigorously boiling distilled water at sea level. Mark the expanded meniscus level as **$100^\circ\text{C}$**.
3. **Linear Graduation:**
   Divide the vertical distance between the $0^\circ\text{C}$ mark and the $100^\circ\text{C}$ mark into **100 equal increments**. Each increment is exactly **$1.000^\circ\text{C}$** ($1.000\,\text{Kelvin}$).
4. **Absolute Zero ($0\,\text{K} = -273.15^\circ\text{C}$):**
   The point at which all atomic thermal motion ceases.

### Step 5: Astronomical Calendar Alignment (Re-deriving the Year)

Without digital calendars, determine the exact 365.2422-day solar tropical year using a vertical gnomon stick:
1. Plant a perfectly vertical 2-meter post in a flat, level field.
2. At local solar noon every day, mark the tip of the shadow on the ground.
3. **Summer Solstice:** The day with the shortest noon shadow marks the longest day of the year (approx. June 21 in Northern Hemisphere).
4. **Winter Solstice:** The day with the longest noon shadow marks the shortest day of the year (approx. December 21).
5. **Spring & Autumn Equinoxes:** The two days halfway between the solstices when day and night are exactly 12 hours long.
6. Counting days from one summer solstice to the next establishes the true year ($365\,\text{days}$ with a leap day every 4 years).
