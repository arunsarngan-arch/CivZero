Digital storage media (hard drives, flash memory, magnetic tapes) degrade within 10 to 30 years without continuous electrical power, clean-room environments, and specialized silicon read heads. If civilization experiences a multi-generational dark age, all computerized knowledge evaporates into bit rot.

The ultimate physical medium of human memory is **acid-free rag paper** impressed with **carbon or iron-gall ink**. Books printed with Gutenberg's movable type in 1455 remain completely legible today, over 570 years later. This manual provides the complete blueprint for manufacturing permanent rag paper, 1,000-year indelible archival ink, casting expandable lead-antimony type alloy, and operating a wooden screw printing press to mass-produce knowledge across a rebuilt world.

## 1. Archival Rag Papermaking Protocol

Modern cheap printing paper is made by grinding whole trees into wood pulp without chemical washing. Residual wood lignin oxidizes into sulfuric and abietic acids, causing modern paper to turn yellow and crumble into dust within 40 to 60 years. 

**True archival paper** is made from pure cellulose derived from **cotton, linen rags, hemp cordage, or mulberry inner bark (Kozo)**. It is chemically neutral ($pH\ 7.5–8.5$) and will not degrade over a thousand years.

1. **Fermentation & Retting:** Cut old linen and cotton rags into 25 mm squares. Soak in water barrels for 2 to 3 weeks until natural bacterial fermentation softens the fibers.
2. **Caustic Cooking:** Boil the rags for 6 hours in a heavy iron pot containing a **$5\%$ Sodium Hydroxide ($NaOH$) or concentrated wood ash lye ($K_2CO_3$)** solution. The alkali dissolves all remaining waxes, starches, and vegetable fats, leaving pure, brilliant white cellulose fibrils.
3. **Maceration / Beating (The Hollander Beater):**
   - The rags must be pounded until the fibers are completely teased apart into microscopic hair-like filaments suspended in water.
   - Use a water-wheel-powered trip hammer (or heavy wooden mortar and pestles) to beat the cooked fiber slurry for **8 to 12 hours**. The result is a smooth, creamy white pulp suspension (*stuff*).
4. **The Mold and Deckle:**
   - Construct a wooden frame stretched with a fine brass wire mesh screen ($30\text{ wires per inch}$).
   - Fit a removable wooden border (the **deckle**) over the screen to hold the liquid pulp on top of the wire.
5. **Forming the Sheet:**
   - Dip the mold and deckle vertically into the warm pulp vat.
   - Pivot horizontally and lift out smoothly.
   - Shake the frame gently forward, backward, left, and right. This cross-directional shake causes the floating cellulose fibers to interlock into a dense, multi-directional felt web as water drains through the wire.
6. **Couching (Transfer):**
   - Remove the deckle rim.
   - Invert the wire screen smoothly onto a wet wool felt sheet, transferring the delicate wet paper sheet without tearing.
   - Stack alternating layers of wet paper and wool felts into a "post" of 100 sheets.
7. **Pressing & Sizing:**
   - Place the post under a heavy wooden screw press. Apply **20 tons of compressive force** to squeeze out $85\%$ of the water.
   - Separate the damp paper sheets and hang them over horsehair ropes in an airy drying loft.
   - **Internal Sizing:** Dip the dry sheets into a warm bath of **dilute animal hide gelatin ($2\%$)** mixed with a pinch of potash alum ($KAl(SO_4)_2$). Gelatin seals the microscopic pores, preventing ink from bleeding or "feathering" when written or printed upon.

## 2. Indelible Iron Gall Ink Formula

Carbon black ink sits on top of paper and can be washed away with water. **Iron Gall Ink** does not sit on the surface; it chemically bonds with the cellulose fibers of the paper:

```
    Tannic Acid (from Oak Gall Nuts)  +  Ferrous Sulfate (Green Vitriol FeSO4)
                                   │
                      (Forms Soluble Ferrous Tannate)
                                   │
                    (Penetrates Deep into Paper Fibers)
                                   │
                  (Atmospheric Oxygen Oxidation on Page)
                                   ▼
             Insoluble Ferric Pyrogallate (Deep Blue-Black)
                  (Permanently Waterproof & Light-Fast)
```

### Ingredients:
- **60 grams Oak Gall Apples:** Spherical, hard parasitic growths found on oak leaves, formed by gall wasps. They contain the highest concentration of natural *gallotannic acid* on Earth ($60–70\%$).
- **30 grams Green Vitriol (Ferrous Sulfate, $FeSO_4 \cdot 7H_2O$):** Derived from vitriol crystallization (Tier 5).
- **30 grams Gum Arabic (or Wild Cherry Tree Resin):** Acts as a viscous suspension binder and gloss agent.
- **500 ml Rainwater or Distilled Water.**

### Brewing Protocol:
1. Crush oak gall apples into a coarse powder with a mortar and pestle.
2. Simmer gently in rainwater for 3 hours to extract gallotannic acid into an amber-brown liquor. Strain out the woody debris.
3. In a separate jar, dissolve the green vitriol crystals in warm water.
4. Pour the vitriol solution into the oak gall tea. **The mixture instantly turns an intense, deep, velvet blue-black!**
5. Stir in the powdered gum arabic until completely dissolved.
6. When applied to paper with a steel pen or quill, it writes as a dark violet-grey. Over the following 24 hours, atmospheric oxygen oxidizes the ferrous ions ($Fe^{2+}$) into ferric ions ($Fe^{3+}$), turning the text into a permanent, indelible, waterproof black pigment that will not fade in sunlight for centuries.

## 3. Casting Movable Metal Type (The Type Mold)

Wood-block printing requires hand-carving every single letter for an entire page into a block of wood—taking weeks per page. Johann Gutenberg's true invention was **reusable, interchangeable, mass-produced metal type**:

1. **The Type Alloy (The Miracle of Antimony):**
   - Pure lead shrinks significantly when cooling, which rounds off the sharp corners of tiny letters, producing blurry, illegible prints.
   - The master type alloy formula: **$70\%\text{ Lead, }15\%\text{ Antimony, }15\%\text{ Tin}$**.
   - **The Physics of Antimony:** Antimony is one of the very few elements on the periodic table that **expands as it solidifies from liquid to solid** (like ice). When poured into a mold, the alloy expands slightly, forcing molten metal into the microscopic serifs of the matrix, reproducing razor-sharp 8-point letterforms.
   - The tin lowers the melting point to **$260^\circ\text{C}$** and increases flow fluidity.
2. **The Adjustable Hand Mold:**
   - A two-piece L-shaped steel mold held together with a spring bow.
   - Sliding the two halves together allows the typecaster to instantly cast letters of varying widths (a wide 'M' vs a narrow 'I') while maintaining perfectly uniform body height (**0.918 inches / 23.3 mm "type high"**).
   - An experienced founder can cast **2,000 to 3,000 individual type sorts per day**.
3. **The Composition Stick:**
   - A typesetter holds a brass composing stick in his left hand, picking individual letters from the type case (capitals from the "Upper Case", small letters from the "Lower Case").
   - Words are separated with lead spaces and assembled upside-down and left-to-right into lines of uniform width.
   - Lines are locked into a heavy iron frame (the **Chase**) using wooden wedges (quoins).

## 4. Constructing & Operating the Gutenberg Screw Press

1. **The Oak Frame:** Built of massive 200 mm square oak timbers securely mortised and braced to the floor and ceiling beams to resist tons of upward thrust.
2. **The Wooden Screw & Box:** Hand-carved from hard, dense beech or hornbeam wood (or cast in bronze), with a steep 50 mm Acme thread. A 2-meter iron lever arm turns the screw through 90°, driving a flat cast-iron plate (the **Platen**) downward with **over 2,000 kg of uniform vertical pressure**.
3. **Oil-Based Printing Ink:** Ordinary water-based writing ink will bead up and slip off greasy metal type. Gutenberg formulated the first **oil-based printing ink**:
   - Boil aged **linseed oil** in an iron kettle until it thickens into a sticky varnish.
   - Grind with pure **lampblack carbon soot** on a stone slab for 3 hours until completely dispersed into a thick, tacky black paste.
4. **The Printing Cycle:**
   - Dab the composed type forme with leather ink balls stuffed with wool.
   - Place a sheet of damp rag paper onto the tympan frame, fold the paper frisket down, and roll the carriage under the platen using a hand-cranked windlass.
   - Pull the screw lever with full body weight. The platen presses the paper flat against the inked type.
   - Release the lever, roll out the carriage, and peel off a crisp, indelible printed page.
   - **Production Velocity:** A two-man press crew produces **250 printed sheets per hour**—a volume equivalent to months of hand-copying by monastic scribes.
